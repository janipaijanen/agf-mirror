```markdown
## Internet Landscape Scan: Siblings, Partners, and Competitors

Status: open TODO (Level 1: Planned). Owner: planner-architect for scope and
comparison axes; fact-checker and contribution-auditor for source verification
and classification; governor-reviewer for any public comparison, partnership
outreach, radar promotion, or binding prioritization.

Run a systematic Internet landscape scan starting from `docs/sources.md` and
expanding through primary project sites, repositories, package registries,
papers, standards communities, and credible ecosystem directories. Identify
projects that may be:

- **siblings** — independently convergent frameworks with similar human gates,
  named roles, durable coordination, evidence, or governed agent workflows;
- **potential partners or complements** — tools or communities whose runtime,
  security, observability, interoperability, research, or distribution surface
  could pair with AGF without duplicating its authority model;
- **competitors or substitutes** — products or frameworks solving materially
  similar adoption, orchestration, review, accountability, or audit problems;
  and
- **prior art** — earlier mechanisms that should shape AGF claims even when no
  current partnership or competitive relationship exists.

MR !962 / `docs/sources.md` Batch 7 is an initial lighter-verification
discovery pass, not completion of this TODO. Reuse its candidates and caveats;
do not repeat the same search as if that work did not exist.

Minimum assessment scope:

- derive search concepts from the existing source registry without sending
  confidential adopter details or unnecessary AGF-identifying context to an
  external provider;
- verify important claims from primary sources, including project activity,
  license, governance model, human approval boundaries, audit-trail design,
  supported agents/runtimes, and CLI/MCP or integration surfaces;
- record the query date, source URLs, evidence status, and confidence limits;
- separate functional similarity from organizational fit: a close competitor
  may be a poor partner, while a narrow complement may offer strong
  interoperability value;
- identify concrete partner hypotheses such as compatible evidence exchange,
  adapters, shared research, cross-evaluation, standards work, or reciprocal
  documentation, without contacting anyone or making commitments;
- compare candidates using stable axes rather than marketing language: human
  authority, role separation, gate enforcement, durable state, provenance,
  security boundary, deployment model, extensibility, and exit/removability;
- distinguish verified facts, source-backed inference, and open questions;
  and
- route any proposed `docs/radar.md` entry, public competitor comparison,
  external outreach, integration, or partnership through its own governed
  plan and human approval.

Candidate output:

1. A verified update to `docs/sources.md` with sibling/partner/competitor/prior-
   art classifications and dated primary-source evidence.
2. A concise landscape matrix showing meaningful similarities, differences,
   complementarity, and unresolved questions.
3. A ranked list of radar candidates, integration experiments, or potential
   partner conversations, each with an explicit benefit, risk, evidence level,
   and required next gate.
4. A repeatable scan cadence and cursor so later passes update the landscape
   instead of restarting from zero.

This TODO authorizes research and assessment only. It does not authorize
external contact, installation, authentication, data transfer, public
comparative claims, partnership commitments, or implementation.
```
