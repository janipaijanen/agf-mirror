## Plain-language progressive disclosure (jgeg amendment)

**Standard:** ELI5 clarity without infantilizing the reader or removing
necessary precision. Concretely, per `jgeg`: start from the reader's
concrete problem or question; explain one concept at a time in ordinary
language; use a short concrete example where it materially improves
understanding; introduce the exact AGF term only after its meaning is
clear; push commands, schemas, maturity vocabulary, and implementation
detail to links or later sections; keep bounded evidence language and
technical accuracy intact — simple wording must not become an absolute
promise or silently drop a limitation.

**Exact file inventory — no others included.** Identified from the
evaluator-facing surface `jgeg` names (homepage, root README, adoption
decision aid, adopter FAQ, use cases, nutshell orientation) against this
project's actual site structure:

| File | Role | Reader question | Next action after reading |
|---|---|---|---|
| `agf/docs/site/index.md` | Public homepage | "What is this, and is it for me?" | Follow "How to adopt" or a "Where to go next" link |
| `agf/docs/site/docs/index.md` | Docs-site landing page (near-duplicate homepage entry, own "Nutshell" info box) | "Where do I start reading?" | Pick a numbered path step (Start / Concepts / Adopt / …) |
| `README.md` (repo root) | Developer-facing repo entry point; `documentation-audience-rule` targets *developers* here, not business evaluators | "What is this repo and how do I run it?" | Quick start / make targets, or link out to `should-you-adopt.md` for the adoption decision itself |
| `docs/start/should-you-adopt.md` | Adoption decision aid | "Should my project actually adopt this?" | Adopt now / wait / skip decision, or read "What adoption asks of you" |
| `docs/start/adopter-faq.md` | Frequently-asked adopter questions | "Does AGF handle situation X?" | Jump to the matching Q&A anchor |
| `use-cases.md` | Fit-for-purpose guidance | "Is my situation a credible AGF use case?" | Match own situation against credible/non-use-case lists |

**Per-file scope note:** the two `index.md` files and the two "Nutshell"
info boxes inside them are treated as one concept surface (they share
near-duplicate prose) — the same plain-language pass applies to both, kept
consistent with each other rather than independently rewritten. The root
`README.md` keeps its developer audience per `documentation-audience-rule`
— this pass makes its existing developer-facing explanations lead with the
reader's problem before jargon, it does not convert the README into
evaluator-facing marketing copy or duplicate `should-you-adopt.md`'s
content.

## One coherent public user outcome

**A first-time evaluator reads the homepage and forms an accurate,
proportionate impression of what AGF is, what it costs, and whether the
project's own internal claims are consistent with each other** — without
being asked to evaluate command-level tooling detail before they've seen
what they get, without reading a data-handling claim the project's own
rules contradict, and without being told every command is equally mature
when the label is doing that work rather than the content. A returning
reader or contributor can tell, at a glance in a GitLab issue or MR thread,
which assistant or human wrote a given note going forward.

**Non-goals:** redesigning site navigation, the full `1a7k` specification,
GitLab service accounts, #53's scope, any of the 11 excluded WARN/INFO
items, a site-wide plain-language rewrite, rewriting operator/reference
pages to beginner level, and any change to technical contracts, schemas,
or commands under the guise of simplification.

**Stop conditions:** if implementing any of the 8 frozen-scope items
surfaces a defect requiring a schema, role, tooling/API, or adopter-contract
change, that finding splits into its own issue and plan — it does not
expand this patch's scope.

## Assistant-attribution byline — implementation detail (revised per s36p BLOCK 2)

**Canonical artifact:** `agf/docs/site/docs/operate/day-to-day.md`, new
subsection under the existing "Coordination hygiene" heading — not a new
page, not an unspecified "process page."

**Convention:** `### <Execution identity> · <AGF role>` (e.g. `### Claude ·
planner-architect`) as the first heading on assistant-authored GitLab
issue/MR notes and descriptions. When a note bridges a durable coordination
record, name that record's ID near the byline.

**Temporal scope, stated explicitly:** this convention is **prospective
only**. It applies to GitLab notes and descriptions filed after this
convention is documented and this MR merges. No historical GitLab note is
edited or expected to comply retroactively — GitLab notes are not files
this project's tooling can rewrite in place the way coordination records
are versioned, and attempting to edit historical platform comments is out
of scope.

**Representative acceptance records:** this implementation MR's own
description and its Gate 2 handoff coordination note are the first two
records produced under the convention, and are the acceptance evidence
named in advance — not a vague "representative timeline" chosen after the
fact.

**Platform boundary, stated on the page:** a byline improves attribution and
readability; it is not authentication or non-repudiation. GitLab's native
author field remains the shared account regardless. Distinct native
author identity requires separate GitLab accounts/credentials, explicitly
out of scope per `3d27`.
