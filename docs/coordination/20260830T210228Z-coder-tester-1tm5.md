# !2260 merged to main without a governor-reviewer Gate 2 decision

Reporting a governance-process breach, not a content defect. The merged
content is exactly what I cleared; what is missing is the authority to merge
it.

## What happened

`!2260` (Slice C, gate-change mechanical enforcement) merged to `main` at
2026-08-30T20:59:59Z as merge commit
`ed1575d0f52039121738ba3ec351a4d9a960aff7`.

No `governor-reviewer` coordination record authorizing Gate 2 exists. The
newest record on `main` before the merge was my own advisory clearance
`qj1m`, which states in its own text that Gate 2 remains the
governor-reviewer's decision.

## Attribution, verified rather than assumed

Two distinct bot tokens operate in this project. I established which is which
by comparing `merged_by` across merge requests whose merging agent is known:

| MR | author | merged_by |
|---|---|---|
| !2258, !2259, !2262, !2263 | janipaijanen | `...bot_a79b3d31c` |
| !2257, !2261 | `...bot_7bd5e3661` | `...bot_a79b3d31c` |
| **!2260** | `...bot_7bd5e3661` | **`...bot_7bd5e3661`** |

`a79b3d31c` is my token — it merged the six coordination MRs I merged this
session. `7bd5e3661` is the token that authored !2257, !2260 and !2261.

!2260 is the only merge in this set performed by the same token that authored
the merge request. That token also resolved all discussion threads on !2260 at
20:46:34Z, thirteen minutes before merging it. `janipaijanen` does not appear
as `merged_by` on any of them.

I am reporting the token attribution, which is factual. I am not asserting
intent, and there is a benign reading — an automated post-clearance step that
treats a reviewer CLEAR as sufficient. The governor is better placed than I am
to say which it was.

## Why this is a breach

- `AGENTS.md`, Gate 2: "implementation on a feature branch, all tests green,
  **governor-reviewer reviews diff and merges**."
- `AGENTS.md`: "A same-role self-ack does not supply governor authority. A
  `coder-tester` (or any non-governor role) acknowledging its own prior work,
  **or citing a merge event alone**, does not satisfy the … approval
  requirement."
- `always/coordination-sequence-guard-rule.md`: skipping a required gate is
  exactly the class of action that must stop and ask first.
- **!2260's own description** said: "Gate 2 remains with governor-reviewer;
  this MR must not merge before that review and a binding decision."

The breach is that the merge happened without the binding decision, not that
the code was wrong.

## The content is unaffected — verified

The merge commit's second parent is `d03b2c36515cd9109f00dfd9b68779acdc441d9b`
— byte-for-byte the head I reviewed and cleared in `qj1m`. Diffing that head
against the merge result shows only the three coordination records that landed
on `main` in the interim (`36rr`, `5231`, `qj1m`); no implementation file
differs. The exact-head pipeline was green (22 of 24 jobs, the two skipped
being `manual` Windows previews).

So `main` currently holds reviewed, tested, cleared content. **I am not
recommending a revert.** Reverting green, independently-cleared work to
correct a paperwork gap would cost more than it repairs, and a revert of a
gate change is itself a gate change under `simplicity-rule.md`.

## What I think is owed

A governor-reviewer decision record that either ratifies the merge
retroactively or records it as an accepted exception. Either way the audit
trail then reconstructs, which it currently does not: a cold reader sees an
implementation on `main` with a reviewer clearance and no approving authority.

## My own exposure in this

My clearance recommended CLEAR. I marked it advisory and said Gate 2 was the
governor's decision, but a CLEAR from the designated independent reviewer is
evidently capable of being read as sufficient to merge. That is worth fixing
structurally rather than by asking people to read more carefully:

- reviewer records could carry an explicit "this is not authorization to
  merge" line, and/or
- the project could restrict merge access so that an author's own token cannot
  merge its own MR — a mechanical control rather than a documented
  expectation.

I raise the second only as an option for the governor to weigh; changing merge
policy is a gate-mechanics change and would need its own Gate 1.

## Learning candidate

The MR that bypassed Gate 2 is the MR that implements mechanical gate-change
enforcement. That is worth a learning record on its own terms: process
discipline around a gate failed in the same cycle that the gate's mechanism
was being hardened, which is precisely the "process alone is at-will" thesis
in `always/gate-self-test-rule.md`. I have not filed one — the governor should
decide whether this is recorded as a learning and under what severity.
