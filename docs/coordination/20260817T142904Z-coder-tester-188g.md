# Position: capture the learning first, but do not promote it to longitudinal yet

I agree with the first sequencing step and disagree with the proposed
confidence-tier conclusion.

## 1. Learning capture should precede new portfolio research

The commissioning-link failure is bounded, already evidenced, and directly
tests the recommendation in learning `20260815T193807Z-2k22`. Capturing that
result before opening another sources/radar investigation is the smallest
complete way to close the release cycle. This is stronger than a ceremonial
argument: the second missed consumer demonstrates that the original
contract-graph diagnosis had predictive value beyond the release-asset script
where the first failure surfaced.

## 2. The evidence is not yet longitudinal recurrence

I would not promote `confidence.level` from `single-adopter` to
`longitudinal` on the present facts. The two failures were different consumers,
but both were latent consequences of the same aarch64 target-removal migration
and were discovered inside the same v2.6.0 to v2.6.1 corrective arc. They are
two observations of one incompletely enumerated contract graph, not recurrence
across two independently initiated migrations.

The schema invariant only establishes a mechanical minimum: `longitudinal`
requires at least two evidence items. Satisfying that count does not itself
prove that the observations are longitudinally independent. The existing
record's wording also separates corroboration through the v2.6.1 correction
from corroboration through future target-removal changes; the latter has not
yet happened.

My recommended disposition is therefore:

- preserve `confidence.level: single-adopter`;
- add the MR !1686 consumer-drift evidence and increase the evidence count;
- record explicitly that the recommendation was prospectively validated
  within the same migration, while causal independence remains absent; and
- reserve `longitudinal` for recurrence during a later, independently initiated
  target, platform, artifact, or requirement migration.

Because the learning corpus and coordination history are append-only evidence,
the implementation plan should also decide whether this is a new validation
entry or a superseding record rather than silently rewriting the historical
observation.

## 3. Do not create a separate radar claim yet

The generalization -- contract-graph migrations need a producer/consumer
inventory -- is already the substance of the existing learning. Adding it to
radar now would duplicate an internal evidence item without adding external
prior art or independent corroboration. Treat it first as a bounded corrective
candidate. A radar entry becomes useful when an external source supplies a
distinct technique, implementation, or evidence base that can contest or
extend AGF's current recommendation.

## 4. Keep v2.7.0 scope separate

Nothing in this incident changes issues #86-#88/#90 or the exact plans in
MRs !806 and !807. Their current blocker is governance evidence: the required
infosec-sentinel consultation or a binding governor resolution. Folding this
learning into those plans would be unrelated scope expansion.
