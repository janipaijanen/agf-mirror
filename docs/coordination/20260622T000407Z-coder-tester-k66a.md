## Answers to the consultation questions

1. **Docs placement.** `roles-and-gates.md` is the right primary home if the
   section stays compact and focuses on authority separation: actor, principal,
   and gate approver are distinct. A new `advanced/` page would be too hidden
   for the first public convention. The FAQ and `should-you-adopt.md` mentions
   are also appropriate.

2. **Convention completeness.** The core shape is correct: human-authored
   grant, AI references via `references-to`, and no AI self-attestation. The
   stated three-field minimum is too thin, though. `principal_kind`,
   `identifier_namespace`, and `identifier` identify who, but not what was
   authorized. For early adopters, `delegation_scope` should be part of the
   minimum convention. `valid_until` should be strongly recommended, and may be
   required for human/service-principal examples, because open-ended delegation
   invites misuse.

3. **Anti-surveillance language.** The framing is accurate and public-docs
   suitable: AGF does not create git surveillance risk from nothing, but
   structured delegation metadata lowers monitoring cost and raises signal
   fidelity. Keep it short in `should-you-adopt.md`; put the richer explanation
   in `roles-and-gates.md` or the FAQ. Do not sound as if AGF solves workforce
   monitoring; the honest claim is that it avoids native surveillance tooling
   and makes the tradeoff visible.

4. **GDPR framing.** The mitigations list is useful, but the current plan's
   phrase about supporting "a defensible GDPR legal basis" is too legalistic
   and may be read as compliance advice. Prefer wording such as: "support local
   privacy, labor, and purpose-limitation review." Also state explicitly that
   team aliases, batch grants, rotating aliases, and retention limits are
   privacy-risk mitigations, not a compliance checklist.

5. **Schema boundary.** Docs-only is the right v2.x boundary. To preserve
   future migration, the docs should mark the convention vocabulary as stable
   but non-enforced: `note_type: delegation-grant`, `delegation`,
   `principal_kind`, `identifier_namespace`, `identifier`, `delegation_scope`,
   `valid_until`, and `cannot_approve`. One wording correction: the plan says
   "the schema may not validate those objects today." The current coordination
   schema appears permissive of additional top-level properties, so the better
   warning is "these objects are not semantically validated or enforced by
   tooling today."

## Recommendation

Approve the direction after the plan validation blocker is fixed.

Content-level changes I recommend before approval:

- Add valid Gate 1 front matter and pass `agov plan validate`.
- Treat `delegation_scope` as minimum convention metadata, not optional
  decoration.
- Recommend or require `valid_until` in examples.
- Replace "defensible GDPR legal basis" with non-advisory privacy/labor review
  wording.
- Clarify that delegation objects may be syntactically accepted but are not
  semantically validated in v2.x.

No implementation should begin until Gate 1 is approved after the plan validates.
