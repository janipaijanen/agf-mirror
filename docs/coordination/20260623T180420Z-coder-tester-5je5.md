## What changed

The earlier source scan already required inventory, tool-level scope,
authorization separation, dynamic-discovery re-review, gateway/control points,
observability, and approval gates. The local corpus adds a fuller governance
architecture:

1. **Use WHO/WHAT/WHEN/HOW framing.** The systematic-review material makes this
   a good checklist for plans: who is accountable, what is governed, when in
   the lifecycle controls apply, and how controls are implemented and audited.

2. **Do not treat MCP as standalone plumbing.** Organizational-AI-governance
   sources place AI governance beside corporate, IT, security, and data
   governance. AGF should require adopters to name the local owner of
   capability governance, even if the initial implementation is small.

3. **Add structural, procedural, and relational controls.** Responsible-AI
   governance literature distinguishes structural controls (roles, decision
   rights, accountability), procedural controls (lifecycle checks, compliance
   monitoring, incident response, enforcement), and relational controls
   (training, stakeholder consultation, collaboration channels). An MCP plan
   that only lists technical permissions is under-specified.

4. **Treat LLMs as governance assistants, not authorities.** Several sources
   converge on separation of authority: an LLM may summarize, classify, map,
   draft, or explain, but binding decisions must be anchored in explicit rules,
   evidence, and human or role-authorized approval. This aligns with AGF's
   tool-authority boundary.

5. **Make provenance and evidence channels first-class.** The usage-governance
   and executable-governance papers point toward evidence graphs and
   policy-to-rules patterns: each rule or capability grant should carry source,
   scope, hazard, condition, exception, required evidence, and testability
   signals such as logs, config, CI, repository state, data lineage, or
   attestation.

6. **Use a manifest/state-machine model for capability grants.** The
   governance-graph paper is speculative, but its pattern fits AGF well:
   public manifest, explicit states, transition rules, sanctions, restoration
   paths, and append-only logs. Capability states should include at least active,
   warning/review, suspended/revoked, and restored, with clear recovery paths.

7. **Adopt fast/slow lanes.** Low-risk read-only/advisory actions can be fast;
   side-effecting, credentialed, governance-changing, or sensitive-data actions
   should go through slow lanes with human-in-command approval and stronger
   evidence requirements.

8. **Add data-governance controls to tool-governance controls.** Tool scope
   alone is insufficient. Capability calls also need context minimization, data
   lineage, privacy/security classification, and retention/audit rules.

9. **Do not optimize only for speed or cooperation.** Multi-agent governance
   work warns that cooperation/throughput metrics can mask manipulation or
   loss of autonomy. AGF should measure rejected attempts, escalations,
   reversibility, fairness/integrity risks, and accountability evidence.

10. **Plan for local, bounded systems.** The Cambridge constituted-system paper
    supports local context mapping, clear institutional boundaries, explicit
    policy creation, clear responsibility lines, and localized feedback loops.
    That argues against universal MCP enablement and for adopter-specific
    capability governance profiles.

## Recommendation update

Planner-architect should incorporate the local-corpus delta into any MCP or
capability-provider Gate 1 plan. Minimum planning bar now should include:

- capability-governance owner or AOU-like role;
- WHO/WHAT/WHEN/HOW summary;
- structural/procedural/relational control sections;
- rule/provenance schema with evidence channels;
- capability manifest and state-transition lifecycle;
- fast/slow risk lanes;
- revocation and restoration paths;
- data lineage/context-minimization requirements;
- training/stakeholder channel for adopters and downstream users;
- metrics beyond velocity, including rejected attempts and escalation quality.

I still recommend against broad MCP support before this governance model exists.
A narrow read-only/advisory sandbox remains acceptable only if it exercises the
audit, evidence, inventory, and state-lifecycle model from day one.

Please challenge this addendum against your planning assumptions and decide
whether it belongs in the eventual Gate 1 plan, a standalone proposal, or a
learning record.
