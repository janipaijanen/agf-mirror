# Lesson proposal: evaluation summaries can anchor self-reinforcing narratives

## Visible proposal

The governor agreed to propose the assessment/meta-assessment learnings and
next steps. The complete public proposal is recorded on issue #62:

https://gitlab.com/janipaijanen/agents-govern-framework/-/work_items/62#note_3595735167

## Bounded observation

Four long external-model assessments were compressed into a four-line ranking
and returned to the assessed services without the complete response set or
Codex rubric. The services mostly accepted their assigned labels and generated
plausible explanations. Later challenges produced more concrete corrections,
but those reactions remained conditioned on the evaluator framing.

This is session-level case evidence. It does not validate the proposed fidelity
model, establish stable provider traits, or prove a general model behavior.

## Proposed learnings

1. Evaluator labels can become self-reinforcing narratives when returned
   without supporting evidence.
2. An assessed model's agreement is a conditioned reaction, not independent
   confirmation.
3. Access to one's own response supports bounded self-description, not
   comparative claims about unseen responses.
4. Evaluation summaries are transformed evidence artifacts whose fitness
   depends on the retained rubric, source responses, claim-level findings, and
   uncertainty.
5. Rank observed response artifacts under recorded conditions, not providers or
   model families.
6. Information loss is not automatically corruption; failure occurs when a
   summary is used beyond the properties it preserves or unsupported detail is
   invented.
7. Initial-answer quality and correction behaviour are separate observations.
8. Evidence availability and verification may differ between evaluator and
   recipient.

Proposed evidence status: **Empirically informed**, confidence
`single-adopter`, based on one multi-service interaction sequence. Treat the
wording as a lesson candidate, not an adopted learning.

## Next proposed steps

When release-priority work and Claude's availability permit:

1. review the issue #62 note and challenge the learning wording;
2. keep #62 at radar maturity and avoid normative schema/rule work;
3. prepare a blinded response-level dossier with exact prompts, responses,
   retrieval disclosures, frozen rubric, claim-level findings, uncertainty,
   derivative ranking, and reactions stored separately;
4. test ranking-only, own-response, own-response-plus-rubric, and full-corpus
   evidence conditions;
5. score self-description separately from comparative claims;
6. pressure-test whether the proposed compact representation-evidence profile
   is usable by a three-person team; and
7. route any paid experiment, governed learning append, or normative
   integration through a narrow Gate 1 plan.

No paid calls, learning append, rule, schema, or implementation is authorized
by this proposal. It must not displace release-priority work.
